\begin{abstract}
% TODO(tac gia): got lai con 150-200 tu truoc khi nop.
Quantum-kernel classifiers are usually evaluated on accuracy alone, yet an
intrusion detector is deployed on the strength of its probabilities. We
benchmark a four-qubit \QSVM{} against Random Forest, XGBoost, an MLP and an
SVM-RBF baseline under one zero-leakage pipeline and one Platt protocol,
measuring Expected Calibration Error and Brier score on the rare
User-to-Root and Remote-to-Local classes. Reliability on rare classes is
easy to mis-measure: on a $\pnTestOld$-record test sample the rare union
holds only $\pnRareOld$ records, and on that sample the quantum kernel
appears to be the best-calibrated model of the five. Measured instead on the
full KDDTest+ split, which holds $\pnRare$ rare records, the ordering
changes. Over $\pnRuns$ paired runs with Holm-corrected Wilcoxon tests, the
quantum kernel is significantly better calibrated than both tree ensembles
($\Delta\ECE_{\text{rare}}=\pdEceRf$, $p_{\text{Holm}}=\pdEceRfHolm$ against
Random Forest), statistically indistinguishable from the classical RBF
kernel, and significantly \emph{worse} than it on Brier score. We also find
that Platt scaling improves calibration for the quantum kernel alone. The
contribution is a reliability result that survives an adequately powered
measurement, and a demonstration of how easily a rare-class calibration
claim does not.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Quantum machine learning, quantum kernel, calibration, reliability,
intrusion detection, NSL-KDD.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A calibration benchmark of a four-qubit quantum kernel against four
        strong baselines under one pipeline and one recalibration protocol,
        on $\pnRare$ rare test records over $\pnRuns$ paired runs with
        Holm-corrected Wilcoxon tests.
  \item The finding that the quantum kernel is significantly better
        calibrated than both tree ensembles on the rare classes, and is
        \emph{not} better than a classical RBF kernel.
  \item The finding that Platt scaling improves calibration for the quantum
        kernel alone, and degrades it for both tree ensembles.
  \item A measurement caution: on a rare union of $\pnRareOld$ records the
        same protocol ranks the quantum kernel first, which the
        $\pnRare$-record measurement does not support.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}

% TODO(tac gia): pipeline, dataset, sieu tham so. Giu nguyen mo ta cua ban cu,
% chi doi ba cho da liet ke trong phan dau file.

All models share the zero-leakage front end of the companion study:
one-hot encoding, \textsc{SelectKBest} to 20 features, PCA to four
components and a min--max map onto $[0,\pi]$. Every transformer is fitted on
the training subset alone. Reliability is evaluated over $\pnRuns$
independent stratified training subsets of $\pnTrain$ records.

Every model, quantum and classical, is trained from scratch on the same
subset. This matters: the submitted version reused cached quantum models
whose training subset differed from the one used to fit their Platt
calibrator, so the quantum and tree branches were not in fact measured on an
identical footing.

\subsection{Test set}
We report on the full KDDTest+ split, $\pnTest$ records of which $\pnRare$
belong to the rare union. The submitted version used a $\pnTestOld$-record
sample containing $\pnRareOld$ rare records, which is too few to estimate a
binned calibration error: at five bins it leaves two records per bin. The
reason for the small sample was the cost of the quantum Gram matrix; with a
closed-form statevector evaluation the full $\pnTest\times\pnTrain$ kernel
takes under a second, so the constraint no longer binds.

\subsection{Statistics}
We report paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with bootstrap confidence
intervals and $d_z$, Holm-corrected within baseline families. Ten runs are
the minimum for which this is meaningful: with five paired runs the smallest
attainable two-sided $p$ is $0.0625$, so no comparison can reach
significance whatever the effect size.

\subsection{Rare-attack reliability}
On the full test set the quantum kernel attains
$\ECE_{\text{rare}}=\pQsvmEceRare\pm\pQsvmEceRareSd$. Against the tree
ensembles the paired difference is $\pdEceRf$
$[\pdEceRfLo,\pdEceRfHi]$ ($p_{\text{Holm}}=\pdEceRfHolm$,
$d_z=\pdEceRfDz$) versus Random Forest and $\pdEceXgb$
$[\pdEceXgbLo,\pdEceXgbHi]$ ($p_{\text{Holm}}=\pdEceXgbHolm$) versus
XGBoost: both corrected-significant in the quantum kernel's favour.

Against the classical kernel the picture is different. On
$\ECE_{\text{rare}}$ the difference is $\pdEceRbf$
$[\pdEceRbfLo,\pdEceRbfHi]$, $p_{\text{Holm}}=\pdEceRbfHolm$ --- the
interval crosses zero and we report it as inconclusive. On
$\Brier_{\text{rare}}$ the classical kernel is ahead by $\pdBrierRbf$
$[\pdBrierRbfLo,\pdBrierRbfHi]$, $p_{\text{Holm}}=\pdBrierRbfHolm$,
$d_z=\pdBrierRbfDz$: corrected-significant \emph{against} the quantum
kernel. We report this as prominently as the wins.

\subsection{What the small test sample did}
Fig.~\ref{fig:testsize} places the two measurements side by side. On the
$\pnRareOld$-record union the quantum kernel records
$\ECE_{\text{rare}}=\pQsvmEceRareOld$ against $\pRbfEceRareOld$ for the RBF
kernel and $\pRfEceRareOld$ for Random Forest, and ranks first of five. The
ranking is not stable at that sample size; the tree-ensemble comparisons

\subsection{Are the tree baselines handicapped?}
The trees share the quantum kernel's four-component representation, which
invites the objection that they are compared in a form that suits them
badly. We therefore repeat them on the $\prefKTwentyDims$ selected features
and on the full $\prefFullDims$ one-hot encoding, holding every
hyper-parameter fixed so that only the representation changes.

More features do not make the trees more trustworthy; they make them less
so. Random Forest's rare-class calibration error rises from
$\prefPcaRfEceRare$ on four components to $\prefFullRfEceRare$ on the full
encoding, and XGBoost's from $\prefPcaXgbEceRare$ to $\prefFullXgbEceRare$.
Ranking moves the other way: Random Forest's AUC-PR improves from
$\prefPcaRfAucPr$ to $\prefFullRfAucPr$ over the same change. The full
representation buys the trees a better ordering and a worse probability,
which is the separation this paper is about. The comparison in
Table~\ref{tab:rare} is therefore not an artefact of a cramped
representation: it is the trees' most favourable calibration setting.

\subsection{When post-hoc recalibration helps}
Platt scaling reduces the quantum kernel's full-set ECE from
$\pQsvmPlattBefore$ to $\pQsvmPlattAfter$ ($\pQsvmPlattDelta$). It is the
only model of the five it helps: Random Forest moves by $\pRfPlattDelta$ and
XGBoost by $\pXgbPlattDelta$, both worse, and even the classical SVM
degrades slightly ($\pRbfPlattDelta$).

The result is not an artefact of choosing a logistic map. Repeating the
comparison with isotonic regression and with temperature scaling gives the
same picture: on the quantum kernel all three recalibrators help and land
within a narrow band ($\pcalQsvmPlatt$, $\pcalQsvmIso$, $\pcalQsvmTemp$
against $\pcalQsvmNone$ uncalibrated), while on both tree ensembles all
three make calibration worse than leaving the native probability alone
(Random Forest $\pcalRfNone$ uncalibrated against $\pcalRfIso$ isotonic).
Post-hoc recalibration on this task is an instrument for the margin-based
quantum kernel and for essentially nothing else.

\subsection{Ranking is not reliability}
The tree ensembles hold the best AUC-PR ($\pXgbAucPr$ for XGBoost,
$\pRfAucPr$ for Random Forest) while holding the worst calibration. Ranking
quality and probability quality are separate axes, and a detector deployed
on a probability threshold is governed by the second.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

% TODO(tac gia): mo rong.
The rare union remains class-imbalanced and the binned estimator remains
sensitive to bin count. Results are on NSL-KDD alone. We evaluate Platt
scaling only; isotonic regression and temperature scaling may suit the tree
families better, and the Platt calibrator here is fitted on the training
split, which is in-sample for models that can memorise it.
