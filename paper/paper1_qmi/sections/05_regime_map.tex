Fig.~\ref{fig:regime-map} places the controlled comparisons on NSL-KDD from
Sec.~\ref{sec:results} in one map. Each cell is a paired comparison of \QSVM{}
with one baseline under one condition, with a verdict from the Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon test within
its baseline family (Sec.~\ref{sec:stats}). One cell compares the kernel--target alignment of the
\ZZ{} and \Zmap{} kernels rather than a classification score (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-ent}). It checks
the mechanism behind the entanglement ablation, so the map shows it with a hollow marker and
the counts below leave it out, which leaves $109$ cells.

The cells do not share one protocol (Table~\ref{tab:protocol}). Forty-two come from the
training-set-size sweep under the natural class prior, scored on the full KDDTest$^+$ set. In
this block $11$ cells favour \QSVM{}, $9$ favour the classical baseline and $22$ are
inconclusive. The other $67$ cells use the rare-enriched training pool: $30$ from the enriched
sweep, scored on the full test set, and $37$ from the entanglement and shift experiments, scored
on test sets of $300$ or $1000$ records. They split $9$, $12$ and $46$. Over all $109$ cells the
count is $20$, $21$ and $68$. A difference estimated on $300$ test records is less precise than
one estimated on $22{,}544$, so we take the natural-prior block as the main result and the full
map as a summary across protocols. Removing test records duplicated in the training data leaves
the counts of the natural-prior block unchanged (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-dedup}).

\subsection{Sensitivity to the multiplicity correction}\label{sec:regime-mult}

Table~\ref{tab:multiplicity} recounts the verdicts from the raw $p$ of each cell under four
corrections. With a Benjamini--Hochberg correction over all $109$ cells the count is $20$, $18$
and $71$. The three cells that change all move from a classical verdict to inconclusive: the
random forest at $N=500$ and $N=1000$ under the natural prior, and XGBoost at a $70\%$ attack
fraction. Every cell that favours \QSVM{} within families also does so under
Benjamini--Hochberg. Within the natural-prior block, the correction over all $109$ cells gives
$11$, $7$ and $24$, since both random-forest cells lie in it; applied to the $42$ cells of that
block alone it changes one cell, giving $11$, $8$ and $23$.

Holm over a whole block rejects no cell, for $109$ cells or for $42$. With ten runs the smallest
two-sided Wilcoxon $p$ is $2/2^{10}=0.00195$, while the first Holm threshold is
$0.05/109=0.00046$ ($0.05/42=0.0012$ for the natural block). This is a limit set by the number
of runs and says nothing about whether the differences are zero; family-wise control over the
whole map would need more runs per cell. In what follows we name the corrections under which
each statement holds.

\subsection{Where the quantum kernel is ahead}

Under the natural prior, \QSVM{} is ahead of XGBoost at $N\geq5000$, of the random forest at
$N=10^4$, and of the linear and polynomial kernels from $N=1000$ and $N=2000$. These verdicts hold
under within-family Holm and under Benjamini--Hochberg. The verdict against XGBoost at $N=5000$
does not survive a wider grid for $C$; the one at $N=10^4$ does (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-n}). All of them are against models given the
same four components. Against XGBoost on all $122$ features no significant difference was
detected at $N=5000$ or $N=10^4$ (Table~\ref{tab:ref-arm}), and full-feature XGBoost trained on
the whole training partition reaches $0.804$, above every four-qubit result. The comparison that
favours \QSVM{} is therefore with classical learners on the same embedding.

On a $50/50$ mixture of normal and DoS traffic, \QSVM{} is ahead of all four kernel baselines,
with effect sizes from $1.56$ to $3.02$, under both corrections; no significant difference was
detected against the tree ensembles. These cells use the enriched pool and a $1000$-record test
set and lie outside the natural-prior block.

The rare-class $F_1$ at $N=10^4$ is $0.507$ for \QSVM{} against $0.334$ for XGBoost and $0.331$
for the random forest, with SVM-RBF higher at $0.585$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-rare}). This comparison
is not a cell of the map.

\subsection{Where it is behind}

Under the natural prior, XGBoost is ahead of \QSVM{} at every $N\leq1000$, by up to $0.081$
macro-$F_1$ at $N=100$, under both corrections. The random forest is ahead at the same sizes
within families, and at $N\leq200$ under Benjamini--Hochberg.

Under feature perturbation all six comparisons of degradation slope favour the baseline, with
effect sizes above $2.8$ in magnitude, under both corrections. On the KDDTest-21 partition the
\Zmap{} control and the linear and polynomial kernels lose less than \QSVM{}. At a $70\%$ attack
fraction XGBoost is ahead within families, but the cell is inconclusive under
Benjamini--Hochberg. All of these cells use the enriched pool and $1000$-record test sets.

Against SVM-RBF, no cell of the training-size sweep favours \QSVM{} under any correction, in
either training pool. The enriched sweep at $N=2000$ favours SVM-RBF.

Outside the map, all $48$ comparisons at eight qubits ($K=80$) favour the baseline, including the \Zmap{} control
(Sec.~\ref{sec:res-width}). These come from noiseless simulation. We did not test other feature
maps, bandwidth scaling or lower-noise hardware, and the map gives no information about them.

\subsection{Reading the map}

Inconclusive cells are the majority. The smallest difference that reached a verdict is $0.010$
macro-$F_1$, while differences as large as $0.031$ stayed inconclusive when they varied widely
between runs. The median absolute difference among the $68$ inconclusive cells is $0.014$. Most
differences in this study are of that size, and ten paired runs resolve only part of them.

The region where \QSVM{} is ahead is bounded on each axis we varied. It needs $N\geq5000$, at $N=5000$ only on the pre-set
grid for $C$, and under the enriched pool we could not sample that far. It holds against learners on the
four-component embedding, not against XGBoost on the raw record. It appears on NSL-KDD; on
UNSW-NB15 the difference against the tree ensembles does not change sign at any $N$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:res-unsw}). An eight-qubit circuit did not extend it. On this evidence, the
four-qubit kernel is a competitive learner on a fixed low-dimensional embedding when training
data are plentiful, and we do not present it as a replacement for a classical detector on the
full feature set.
